# ✨ Features (details)

[Back to README](../../README.en.md) · [日本語](../features.md)

This document explains the key points of "✨ Features" in the [README](../../README.en.md) in detail, including how things look on screen and what goes into the record. The CSV columns and formats are in the [CSV document](csv.md), and how measurement works is in the [measurement document](measurement.md).

## Screen display (numbers, graph, bands)

- **Real-time level visualization**  
  Uses the Web Audio API to graph and display the microphone input level (dBFS) instantly. The horizontal axis of the graph is real time (the last 60 seconds), with ticks and labels. The line draws the very intervals recorded in the CSV, so the graph has no gaps even when drawing on the screen stops. Time when nothing was being recorded and intervals where no sample arrived (missing data) are not joined by the line, and intervals of digital silence stick to the bottom. The value of the ultrasonic band (18–22 kHz) is overlaid as a dashed line, and the legend sits below the graph (the lines can be told apart by both color and line style). ⚠**The big number on screen is the full-band RMS of the latest 2048 samples (about 43 ms at 48 kHz; it depends on the sample rate), so its window differs from that of the `dbfs` in the high-precision mode CSV (the energy average over the whole interval).** For sounds that get loud now and then (a fan's hum, a washing machine and so on), the energy average is pulled up by the loud moments, so the big number looks lower than the CSV value for longer stretches. In fallback mode, the CSV `dbfs` is also this window's value at the moment of recording.
- **Recording and displaying the ultrasonic band (18–22 kHz)**  
  For each interval, records the energy of the ultrasonic band (18,000–22,000 Hz) and of the audible band (20–18,000 Hz) (the band columns in the CSV). On screen, the ultrasonic band value appears as the dashed line in the graph and as "Ultrasonic max" in the statistics. Below the big number, the value of the last recorded interval is shown as a number, as in "Ultrasonic band (last 1 s interval): -91.7 dBFS" (the same as `band_ultra_dbfs` in the CSV; the interval length is the log interval). Both the window and the band differ from those of the big number. Small changes of a few dB are hard to read on the graph, so read them from this number. Until the first interval arrives it shows `--.- dBFS`; for an interval without a value it adds "(no value for this interval)"; and with `?bands=off`, in fallback mode, or at a sample rate that cannot measure the ultrasonic band, a reason appears instead of a value. ⚠**A band value records whether there was sound energy in that band; it cannot tell what the sound is.** For intervals without an ultrasonic band value (fallback mode, `?bands=off`, intervals with no frames usable for the computation), the dashed line breaks. Once recording starts, "Recordable limit on this device: about NN kHz" appears below the legend. It is half of the smaller of the AudioContext and microphone audio track sample rates (if the track does not report its rate, half of the AudioContext rate, with "track rate unknown" added). This limit comes from the sample rate; it does not mean the microphone can pick up sounds that high. The recording notes show the number of intervals whose band valid ratio (`band_valid_ratio`) fell below 1.0 and the minimum, a mismatch between the microphone and AudioContext sample rates (the conversion records high sounds near the limit lower), a sample rate that cannot measure the ultrasonic band, that band computation is stopped with `?bands=off`, and that bands are not computed in fallback mode. Opening the page with `?bands=off` stops band computation (it is for comparing the valid sample ratio with and without bands), and the legend and the notes say that it is stopped. When the ultrasonic band line is not drawn (`?bands=off`, fallback mode, a sample rate that cannot measure the ultrasonic band), the sample line in the legend is hidden and the reason for not drawing it is shown.

## Recording and statistics

- **Recording without missing intervals (AudioWorklet)**  
  Intervals are aggregated on the audio thread, so recording continues even when drawing on the screen stops. One CSV row corresponds to one interval. Where the AudioWorklet cannot be loaded, the tool switches to fallback mode automatically.
- **Automatic statistics**  
  Elapsed time, log rows, average, maximum and minimum level, range, sample peak and Ultrasonic max are computed and displayed automatically. The average is an energy average (the same definition as Leq), and its population matches the CSV rows (excluding intervals where no sample arrived, that is, rows whose `dbfs` is empty). **The average is weighted by interval length (seconds), not by row count.** The log interval can be changed during recording, so averaging 1-second and 3-second intervals with equal weight gives a value that is not proportional to real time. The steps to get the same value from the CSV are in "Analysis steps in Excel/Google Sheets" in the [CSV document](csv.md). At a boundary where recording was stopped and resumed, the difference of `timestamp` includes the whole pause. Only for that row (listed in the trailer's `# sessionStartAt=`), do not use the difference; use the log interval from `# intervalSec=` as the weight. "Ultrasonic max" is the maximum of `band_ultra_dbfs`, and it also counts band values attached to digital-silence rows (see "Band columns" in the [CSV document](csv.md)). In fallback mode and with `?bands=off`, it cannot be measured and stays `--.-`.
- **Showing whether there are recording gaps, both ways**  
  If there are intervals that clipped, the number of those intervals and the total number of clipped samples are shown on screen as they happen; if there are intervals whose valid sample ratio fell below 1.0, their count and the minimum are shown. Clipping is described as single hits or sustained runs. In an interval where 3 or more samples in a row clipped, the waveform is flattened and the value differs from the real sound; single hits of 1–2 samples have little effect on the interval's value. The share of all recorded samples is shown too (the total count alone would look alarming). Even when there are no gaps, it says "No recording gaps (clipping in 0 intervals; valid sample ratio 1.000 in 10 of 10 intervals)". If only one side were shown, the screen would say nothing when the record is clean, and users could not tell "no gaps" from "not checked yet." Rows recorded in fallback mode cannot be measured either way, so the screen says that the gaps cannot be checked, with the number of those intervals.

## Record contents and CSV

- **Privacy: no audio recording**  
  The audio itself is not saved; only level data is handled. The CSP (`connect-src 'none'`) stops sending data outside, as a browser feature.
- **CSV log output (v3)**  
  Exports timestamped level data together with the interval sequence number, peak, clip count, valid sample ratio, band values (the ultrasonic band, 18,000–22,000 Hz, and the audible band, 20–18,000 Hz) and a hash. A band value records whether there was sound energy in that band; it cannot tell what the sound is. The measurement conditions remain in meta lines that start with `#`.
- **Revealing corruption and loss (hash chain)**  
  Each row's hash includes the previous row's hash. When the CSV arrives as it was, you can find accidental corruption, partial loss and reordering. ⚠**It does not withstand intentional changes, whoever makes them** (how the chain is built is published, so rewriting values and recomputing the hashes passes the check; see "What the hash chain can tell you" in the [CSV document](csv.md)).
- **Keeping silence in the record**  
  Digital silence is kept as a row with `-Infinity`. Because no row is dropped, the record can show that "there was no activity." For intervals where no sample arrived (missing data), `dbfs` is left empty to separate them from silence.
- **Noticing microphone disconnection and clock jumps**  
  If the microphone is lost during recording, recording stops and the screen shows up to which row the log is valid. Muting at the source (for example, by an incoming call) and clock drift caused by AudioContext suspension are also shown on screen as they happen.
- **Recording measurement conditions and warnings**  
  After requesting that audio processing on the microphone side (AGC, noise suppression, echo cancellation) be turned off, the tool checks what actually happened with the real values from `getSettings()`. If processing is still on, the screen says that "the dBFS values in this record cannot be treated as absolute." The device name, sample rate and processing state are also kept in the CSV meta lines.

## Operation and display settings

- **Cancelling the microphone request**  
  Leaving the permission dialog alone does not freeze the screen. It times out after 20 seconds, and "Stop" cancels right away even while connecting.
- **Changing settings in real time**  
  The log interval (with 1s/3s/5s/10s/1m presets) and the display floor can be changed during recording and apply immediately. The display floor is a setting for the display; recorded values do not move. The default display floor is -110 dBFS, or -90 dBFS when opened with `?bands=off`. The ultrasonic band in a quiet room can be lower than -90 dBFS, and with a display floor of -90 the dashed ultrasonic band line would stick to the bottom.
- **Cumulative data recording**  
  Repeating "Start recording ⇒ Stop ⇒ Start recording" keeps accumulating the statistics and log data ("Reset stats" clears them manually; it can be pressed after stopping recording and clears the log, statistics, graph and notes at once). However, the log exists only in the browser's memory and disappears when the tab is closed.
- **Multi-device support**  
  Responsive design that fits the screen widths of PCs, tablets and phones (the only real device tested is one iPhone 18 Pro Max, and Android has not been tested; for details, see "Browser support" in the [README](../../README.en.md)). "Start recording" and "Stop" appear in the same place, and only the one you can press is shown ("Stop" cancels while connecting to the microphone). At 480 px wide or narrower, "Export CSV" and "Reset stats" are grouped under "More", and Start recording/Stop, "More", Help and the theme switch sit on one row ("More" also closes with the Esc key).
- **Starts right away, no preparation**  
  Just open the page and allow microphone access to start. A web app with nothing to install.
- **Intuitive user interface**  
  The large number display, color meter, live graph and help make it usable without specialist knowledge.
- **Dark mode and light mode**  
  The theme can be switched to suit your environment (the setting is saved automatically).
- **Switching the display between Japanese and English**  
  The button at the top right of the header (`EN` on the Japanese display, `JA` on the English display) switches the screen text (buttons, statistics, notes, status display, help, tooltips and descriptions for screen readers) between Japanese and English. The initial language is decided in this order: `?lang=ja` / `?lang=en` in the URL → the language chosen with the button and saved → the browser language (Japanese if the first entry of `navigator.languages` is Japanese, otherwise English). You can switch during recording; the recording, statistics, graph and hash chain do not change (only the display changes). The CSV contents and the exported file name are the same regardless of language. How to use it is in "Switching the display language" in the [README](../../README.en.md).
